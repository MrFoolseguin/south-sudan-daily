# South Sudan From the Inside: 2026-11-20

## 1. The Big Picture
Political tension around South Sudan’s upcoming elections is mounting as legal challenges against the National Elections Commission mount and internal party primary feuds spill into the open. On the ground, security vulnerabilities remain high, highlighted by renewed clashes between the SSPDF and SPLA-IO in Tambura County and targeted violence against political figures in Kapoeta. Economically, Juba is making a major push to attract foreign capital by opening 14 oil blocks and establishing national accounting standards, even as Malaysian energy giant Petronas exits the country. Meanwhile, diplomatic relations with the United States remain complicated, marked by a US court decision overturning visa bans on one hand, and sharp disputes over sanctions and US deportee treatment on the other.

## 2. Dinka Heritage Lesson: Cattle Taxonomy and Identity (*Thön & Piny*)
In Muonyjang culture, cattle are far more than livestock; they form the bedrock of social structure, language, and personal identity. The Dinka language features an intricate taxonomy classifying cattle by coat patterns, horn shapes, and shades. Terms such as *Mayom* (white forehead), *Malou* (black and white pattern), *Maniang* (yellowish-brown), and *Yar* (predominantly white) convey specific visual details recognized instantly across cattle camps.

During initiation into adulthood, a young man receives an ox-name (*wööddhe*) derived from the color traits of his favored ox (*muor*). Men use these ox-names in poetry, formal greetings, and songs during social gatherings (*lööc*). This cattle-centric vocabulary serves as a primary source of metaphor in Dinka literature and everyday speech, shaping how individuals express honor, resolve disputes, and maintain clan heritage.

## 3. Local Intelligence
* **Renewed SSPDF–SPLA-IO Clashes in Tambura:** Over 300 internally displaced persons (IDPs) fled their shelters following a resurgence of fighting between the South Sudan People’s Defence Forces (SSPDF) and the SPLA-IO in Tambura County, Western Equatoria *(Eye Radio)*.
* **SPLM Primary Dispute in Aweil:** The SPLM national secretariat publicly accused Presidential Adviser Hussein Abdelbaggi Akol of meddling in ongoing party primaries in Aweil, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, creating political friction within the ruling party *(Sudans Post)*.
* **Kapoeta Security Force Killings:** Kapoeta South County Commissioner Yusif Lobai confirmed that security forces arrested and subsequently killed Kapoeta’s SPLM secretary, Alex Lotyem, and his friend Matthew Loteyo at a local hotel *(Eye Radio)*.
* **Petronas Exits Oil Sector:** Malaysian state oil company Petronas has withdrawn from South Sudan’s oil sector under undisclosed terms, leaving a major gap just as the Ministry of Petroleum opens 14 new exploration blocks to investors *(Eye Radio, Sudans Post)*.
* **Public Accounting Institute Launched:** The Ministry of Finance and Planning, alongside the World Bank, officially established the Institute of Certified Public Accountants of South Sudan (ICPASS) to professionalize accountancy and improve public financial management *(Eye Radio)*.
* **Juba Commissioner Challenges Foreign Investors:** Juba County Caretaker Commissioner Maulano Kalisto Lado publicly criticized foreign businesses operating in Juba for paying low wages to local workers while extracting significant profits *(Eye Radio)*.

## 4. Rumors and Reactions
* **Treatment of US Deportees in South Sudan**
  * **Claim:** International news outlets report that deportees sent from the US to South Sudan faced starvation, extortion, and guard abuse *(NYT, Arab News, ABC News)*.
  * **Reaction:** South Sudanese officials rejected the claims, denying that deportees are subject to hunger or inhumane conditions *(TRT Afrika)*.
  * **Label:** *Unverified / Contested*. International outlets cite testimonies from families and legal advocates, while local government sources deny mistreatment. Independent verification by local human rights monitors in Juba is needed to confirm the true conditions of detention.

## 5. Economy and Money
* **Oil Sector:** The caretaker Petroleum Minister offered 14 exploration blocks to foreign investors, aiming to raise crude output above 200,000 barrels per day within two years *(Sudans Post)*. Meanwhile, Petroleum Undersecretary warned oil cartels against illicit interference *(Eye Radio)*, and Petronas announced its exit from the country *(Eye Radio)*.
* **Financial Oversight:** The launch of ICPASS creates a formal framework for corporate and public auditing *(Eye Radio)*.
* **Prices & Inflation:** Traders and families in Juba’s Gudele market report rising food prices cutting into household budgets *(Eye Radio)*. 
* *Exchange Rate Note:* Sudan’s currency dropped to 12,000 Sudanese pounds per USD in Khartoum *(Sudan Tribune)*, but no reliable exchange rate figures for the South Sudanese Pound (SSP) were reported in today's sources.

## 6. Land and Property
* **Suspension of Illegal Demarcations:** Juba County Caretaker Commissioner Maulano Kalisto Lado suspended all illegal land demarcation activities across Juba County to curb land grabbing and unregulated sales *(Eye Radio)*.
* **Mining Transparency:** The US Embassy raised formal concerns regarding a lack of transparency in South Sudan’s mining sector regulatory framework *(Eye Radio)*.
* **International Investment Talks:** South Sudanese delegates held high-level investment discussions in Riyadh with Saudi Arabian counterparts *(Eye Radio)*.
* *Verification Guidance:* Prior to buying land or property in Juba County, verify boundaries and ownership records directly with the Juba County Commission and the national Ministry of Housing. Illegal demarcations are subject to immediate executive suspension.

## 7. Region and the United States
* **United States:** A US federal court overturned the Trump administration's immigrant visa ban affecting South Sudan and 25 other African nations *(Eye Radio)*. However, official relations remain strained; the Foreign Ministry rejected recent US sanctions *(Eye Radio)*, while a US envoy warned South Sudanese leadership of impending "consequences" over political stagnation *(Eye Radio)*.
* **Sudan Conflict:** Cross-border instability persists. Fighting in Sudan between the SAF and RSF has expanded in Blue Nile State, with UN reports indicating drone strikes cause nearly three-quarters of war deaths this year *(CNBC Africa, Sudan Tribune)*.
* **Uganda Border:** The Elegu border post between South Sudan and Uganda has reopened, stabilizing land trade routes *(Eye Radio)*.

## 8. Opportunities to Watch
* **Public Accounting & Audit Services:** The creation of ICPASS will require businesses, NGOs, and public institutions to align financial reporting with formal accounting standards. Building localized bookkeeping or auditing capabilities will be essential for future business operators.
* **Downstream Energy & Logistics:** With Petronas exiting and the government promoting 14 oil blocks alongside refinery plans, logistics and legal services in the energy sector face significant shifts.
