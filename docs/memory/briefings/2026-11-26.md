# South Sudan From the Inside: 2026-11-26

## 1. The Big Picture
President Salva Kiir has taken explicit steps to lower political and commercial friction across the country by ordering security forces to allow free movement for political actors ahead of the 2027 elections and to eliminate illegal checkpoints hindering aid and diplomatic transport. At the same time, opposition parties are demanding strict security neutrality, and civil society is pressing to extend voter registration deadlines. On the economic front, the national government is pushing aggressive energy targets—seeking to double short-term oil production and renew key Asian partnerships—while central banking and municipal authorities step up enforcement against unlicensed forex dealers and urban sanitation violators.

## 2. Dinka Heritage Lesson: Cattle Naming and Personal Identity (*Muör* and *Wöu*)
In traditional Muonyjang (Dinka) society, cattle are far more than economic assets; they are fundamentally tied to personal identity, lineage, and social expression. When a young man is initiated into his age-set (*ric*), he receives a designated bull (*muör*) from his family. From the physical markings, coat patterns, and horn shapes of this bull, he derives his personality name or praise name (*wöu*).

The Dinka language possesses a rich vocabulary to classify coat patterns—such as *majok* (a black body with a white head and spots), *yar* (predominantly white), *mabor* (white-faced), or *malith* (ash-grey). A young man composes poetry and praise songs dedicated to his bull, reciting them during community dances, assemblies, and social gatherings. This tradition creates a lifelong symbolic bond between the individual, his herd, and his clan. For anyone looking to understand Dinka social life, recognizing how cattle imagery structures personal pride, poetry, and respect is essential.

## 3. Local Intelligence
* **President Kiir Orders Unhindered Movement for Aid and Political Campaigns:** President Salva Kiir signed Republican Orders instructing security personnel to cease mounting illegal roadblocks, halt unauthorized tax collection on humanitarian and diplomatic consignments, and guarantee free movement for political parties and candidates *(Sudans Post, Eye Radio)*. *Why it matters:* Bureaucratic and security roadblocks significantly inflate transport costs for businesses and humanitarian relief while creating political intimidation ahead of the February 2027 polls.
* **SPLM Primaries Conclude in Key Districts:** In Lakes State, Agok Manyiel Dhieu secured the SPLM nomination for governor after defeating former governor Madhang Majok Meen by five votes. In Rumbek East, Ambassador Malek Ruben secured the nomination for his parliamentary constituency *(Sudans Post, YouTube)*. *Why it matters:* Party primaries highlight competitive internal dynamics within the ruling party ahead of candidate nomination deadlines.
* **Machar Testifies Regarding Wartime Evacuations:** Former First Vice President Dr. Riek Machar testified before a special court, denying that he ordered the late Major General David Majur Dak to surrender. Machar stated he had requested two helicopters from the Office of the President and the United Nations to evacuate 219 personnel trapped in Nasir *(Sudans Post, Eye Radio)*. *Why it matters:* Signals ongoing judicial proceedings concerning historical military actions and political command structures.
* **Media Development Institute Cleared to Resume Operations:** South Sudan’s Media Authority lifted its administrative suspension on the Media Development Institute (MDI) following instructions from Information Minister Ateny Wek Ateny *(Sudans Post, Eye Radio)*. *Why it matters:* Reopens a primary local institution responsible for training professional journalists ahead of national election reporting.
* **Highway Ambush and Intercommunal Clashes Recorded:** Gunmen opened fire on a passenger vehicle on the Juba-Mundri road in the Biyokoriwa area, injuring two people *(Eye Radio)*. Concurrently, intercommunal clashes between Rumbek Centre and Cuibet County in Lakes State left seven people dead *(Eye Radio)*. *Why it matters:* Persistent road ambushes and rural violence highlight lingering security risks along key supply routes.

## 5. Economy and Money
* **Oil Targets and Partner Renewals:** The Ministry of Petroleum is targeting a short-term crude output of 200,000 barrels per day (bpd), with a long-term goal of 400,000 bpd and 50% national power access by 2035 *(Pan African Visions, Oil & Gas Middle East)*. The government officially renewed operational oil agreements with partners from China and Malaysia *(Yeni Safak English)*. President Kiir acknowledged in public remarks that oil revenues have not yet transformed the living standards of citizens *(Radio Tamazuj)*.
* **Power Deficit:** South Sudan currently ranks last in Africa in grid electricity access, with only 5.4% of the population connected *(DNE Africa)*.
* **Import Costs and Local Inflation:** The country continues to spend an estimated $800 million annually on food imports, while traders in Juba's Gudele market report that rising consumer prices are severely cutting into purchasing power and business volume *(Eye Radio)*.
* **Forex Regulation:** The Bank of South Sudan (BoSS) issued a strict warning to close all unlicensed foreign exchange dealers *(Sudans Post)*.
* **Grassroots Development Funding:** Local government structures launched a $150 million grassroots services delivery initiative *(Eye Radio)*.
